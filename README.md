# Ethical Marketing and Consumer Decision-Making

## Overview

This undergraduate research project examines the relationship between ethical marketing practices and consumer decision-making.

The study uses primary survey data to explore how factors such as brand transparency, ethical marketing, price sensitivity, willingness to pay more, and social influence relate to consumer choices.

## Research Methodology

- **Data collection:** Google Forms survey
- **Sample size:** 200 respondents
- **Data analysis tool:** Microsoft Excel
- **Statistical methods:** Mean, median, standard deviation, and Pearson correlation
- **Data visualisation:** Excel charts

## Variables Examined

The survey examined respondents' perceptions and decision-making across areas including:

- Environmental practices
- Brand transparency and trust
- Ethical marketing and purchasing decisions
- Preference for cheaper products
- Ethical product choice
- Willingness to pay more for ethical products
- Importance of price
- Social influence

## Key Findings

The descriptive analysis showed that price importance had the highest mean score among the survey items, while willingness to pay more had the lowest mean.

Pearson correlation analysis showed a positive association between brand transparency and ethical marketing-related purchasing decisions. Other selected relationships showed weak or negligible linear associations.

The findings suggest that consumer decision-making around ethical products involves multiple factors rather than a single variable.

## Limitations

- The study is based on self-reported survey responses.
- The sample may not represent the wider population.
- The study uses cross-sectional data collected at one point in time.
- Correlation analysis identifies associations but does not establish causation.

## Project Outputs

The repository contains the research report, survey questionnaire, Excel analysis, and visualisations used in the study.
